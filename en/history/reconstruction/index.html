A photographic report by Emil Weber

The twinning and the building of a memorial

From the first idea in the summer of 1981 to the finished chapel in the spring of 1985

  1. Home
  2. History
  3. Reconstruction

Summer 1981

It all began during my holiday, which I spent close to the border with the Bohemian Forest. On outings with my family we passed several memorials built by Bohemian Forest Germans to remind following generations of the suffering of war and expulsion. Since I had taken on the community representation for Obermoldau, from those days on the idea would not leave my head of erecting such a memorial for our home community too. I found the right place for it in Philippsreut, a small municipality in the Bavarian Forest through which the paths of our countrymen had already led before the expulsion. I went to the municipal office there and put to the municipal secretary the proposal of a twinning between Obermoldau and Philippsreut, together with the erection of a memorial.

Autumn 1981

Since the municipal secretary showed himself not unwilling to consider this proposal, I sat down as soon as my holiday was over and put my request to the municipal administration in a letter of 27.9.1981 with the following wording:

… The former parish community of Obermoldau has a great request to make of the municipality of Philippsreut concerning a twinning between Obermoldau and Philippsreut, together with the erection of a memorial …

From the letter to the municipal administration of Philippsreut, 27.9.1981

There followed on 8.10.1981 the following reply:

… The municipal council of Philippsreut has taken note of your letter and welcomes the intention of the former parish community of Obermoldau. We are in principle willing to enter into a twinning with you. Concerning the erection of a memorial in Philippsreut we have already made contact with the Higher Forestry Directorate and will, if this is possible, purchase the plot of land envisaged …

Reply from the municipality of Philippsreut, 8.10.1981

Spring 1982

So the first step had been taken. Things continued on 17 April with the first official meeting between the community representation of Obermoldau and the mayor, Otto Damasko, in Philippsreut. The main item on the programme was the inspection of the plot of land envisaged at the B 12 road junction towards Mauth and in the direction of the Czechoslovak border crossing. At the convivial gathering that followed at Mayor Damasko’s house – he was celebrating his 59th birthday that day – everyone in that cordial circle agreed that a twinning relationship could only be welcomed.

For the first time our undertaking was now also reported in the three Heimatbriefe (newsletters for the expelled communities), with an appeal for donations under the motto „Memorial Obermoldau“.

Summer 1982

During that summer the following proposals came in for the memorial:

  • A stone structure with the names of the places of our parish community
  • The building of an octagonal chapel
  • A replica of the St John Nepomuk Chapel standing in Obermoldau
  • A reduced version of the parish church of Obermoldau
  • A larger artificial stone with the names of the places of the community.

A further burden on the decision then seemed to be the refusal of the Passau road construction office, which did not approve the building of a memorial on the plot envisaged. Yet everything turned out for the best, since with the present site of the new Tusset Chapel we obtained a suitable place.

Autumn 1982

In order finally to reach a decision we came together in Philippsreut for the second time on 9 October. There we also inspected the new plot, with which all those present were very taken. Since the architect found none of the proposals mentioned suitable, and I saw in the design he presented no relation to the old homeland, at this meeting too no decision seemed likely to be reached.

The architect suggested making a faithful replica of a chapel which had stood, or still stood, in the Bohemian Forest homeland. Then the Tusset Chapel came to my mind. I happened to have a picture of it with me, which I passed round. This proposal did not find favour with everyone present; however, after it had been published in the three Heimatbriefe, it did with the majority of our countrymen. Letters and the arrival of numerous donations confirmed my final resolve to rebuild the Tusset Chapel faithfully in Philippsreut.

Winter 1982/1983

Now a countryman still living in the old homeland was asked to take measurements of the Tusset Chapel. In December I received from him a very detailed drawing with the exact dimensions, on the basis of which our architect, Dipl.-Ing. Gerhard Edlmann, drew up the planning applications and submitted them for approval in the spring. Since the exact window dimensions were given, I was able to finish the windows over the winter.

Spring 1983

In order to make an exact reconstruction possible, our architect and I drove to the old Tusset Chapel for the first time in April. There we were occupied for a total of four hours with measuring and photographing.

Summer 1983

Now the greatest part of the work fell to me. During the works holiday, more than 20 cubic metres of building timber were worked at the Ekstein sawmill in Uhingen (where I had been employed for four years as a carpenter and stair builder). A great help to me in the carpentry and planing were my countryman Gustl Tahedl (Guthausen), my wife and my sons, as well as two pensioners who helped with the impregnating.

Autumn 1983

In September – the foundations were already finished – the architect and I drove to the old Tusset Chapel in the Bohemian Forest for a second time. The last uncertainties arising from the evaluation of photographs and drawings were cleared up.

Winter 1983/1984

The carpentry work in Uhingen was completed, and a covered store was built for the worked beams and boards. At my home the shutters and the timber decorations for the interior were finished.

Spring 1984

Before the last snow had melted, work went cheerfully on with fresh strength. The last thing to be done was to turn the oak columns which adorn the entrance front of the Tusset Chapel. On 31 May (it was Father’s Day) everything was ready. All morning I was busy loading with my sons and other countrymen. At exactly midnight on 1 June the lorry – which I had been given free of charge, complete with driver, by the firm where I had been employed until my retirement – set off towards Philippsreut, where it arrived at 7 a.m.

That same morning, since the foundations and plinth were already finished, the raising was begun, and within two days a large part of the timber framework was standing. In our sheer eagerness during the raising I had rather neglected the safety measures, and so it was like a further miracle of Our Lady of Grace of the Tussetberg that when the front wall collapsed, none of us came to any harm. Not even the wall itself was damaged. After four weeks of hard work we were glad that the timber structure of the new chapel was finished, apart from some minor work.

That everything went so smoothly we owe above all to Mayor Damasko, who put up me and Gustl Tahedl and who organised much to keep the work running smoothly. Electricity was made available to us by the neighbours Friedsam and Stumvoll; we were allowed to use the nearby Springer joinery at any time. Father Max Richtsfeld also contributed to the success of our undertaking, for he gave it his spiritual blessing and always had a crate of beer to spare for his thirsty souls. The municipal workers were at our disposal at all times.

Summer 1984

In July I made the journey from Uhingen to Philippsreut yet again. I was accompanied by three Swabian Flaschner (in Bavaria they are called Spengler – tinsmiths). With them and some municipal workers the copper roof was fitted within two days. As always, I stayed with the three craftsmen at „godfather“ Damasko’s.

Autumn 1984

For a second time people believed that a miracle had occurred in the chapel. During a great storm in November a window of the stone chapel which had not yet been firmly anchored fell inwards, frame and all, onto the concrete floor. When it was found, neither the glass nor the frame was damaged.

Winter 1984/1985

In January a miracle happened once again.

We succeeded in bringing the image of Our Lady of Grace of the Tussetberg to Philippsreut, in order to give it a home again in the new Tusset Chapel.

The preparatory work for the solemn consecration and for the design of the commemorative publication was shared with me by the members of the festival committee in Philippsreut; Rosl Tahedl of Guthausen, who lived in the Bohemian Forest until 1964 and had a close relationship with the Tusset Chapel and the miraculous image, was able to contribute a great deal on the recent history of the Tusset Chapel; Otto Veith of Wallern took charge of the design of the commemorative publication.

Spring 1985

With the making of the faithfully reproduced wooden pews and of the altar, my work on the new Tusset Chapel was essentially completed.

With the building of the new Tusset Chapel in Philippsreut, a piece of the Bohemian Forest homeland has come into being again. Many industrious hands of countrymen and of the municipality of Philippsreut contributed to its success. Over 30 square metres of timber were worked in the process. I hope that with my descriptions and with the pictures I have set down the making of the new Tusset Chapel in Philippsreut as a lasting memory.
